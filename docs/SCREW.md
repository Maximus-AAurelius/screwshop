# SCREW workspace

**New here?** Press **▶ WALK ME THROUGH A SCREW MIX** at the top of the tab: 18 steps from loading a record on both tables to a recorded two-song mix (slow, tape sound, record, run-backs, lag + flick, drag, scratch, cue record 2, match tempo, blend, save). Each step lights up the control and moves on when you do it; **SHOW ME** does it for you and **AUTO-PLAY** plays the whole thing with demo records if your library is empty. While a step is being done for you, **SHOW ME** becomes **■ STOP**. Press it (or **Esc**) to take over at once; Esc again closes the card. **❚❚ PAUSE AUTO-PLAY** stops the step it is on. The page only scrolls when a control is actually off screen.

**One screen.** On a laptop or desktop (1100 px wide, 600 px tall or more) the tab works like Serato or rekordbox. Both waveforms are on top, with deck A | mixer | deck B under them. Each deck has the platter and chop pads side by side, with CUE/PLAY, SLOW and the slow/drag/tape sliders under them. SCREW STATION scrolls in its own column on the right. The banner and global transport bar are hidden here; each deck has its own PLAY. Checked at 1366×768, 1440×900 and 1920×1080. Phones keep the stacked layout.

Two tabs: **SCREW A SONG · TABLES** (the screw station) and **VOCALS + INSTRUMENTAL** (stem editor).

## Tables: the screw station

1. **Record.** Pick a saved song → **LOAD ON BOTH TABLES**, or **LOAD SONG FILE**. The same record goes on table A and table B, the way DJ Screw worked with two copies. The tempo is detected on load (BPM shown big). If the song has a completed vocals/instrumental split, it loads too.
2. **Speed.** The pitch fader slows tempo and pitch together, like a turntable, and glides instead of jumping. Presets −10/−20/−30%, or type a target BPM. "Both tables move together" keeps A and B at the same speed. **HOLD TO DRAG** (or hold `D`) is a hand on the platter: the live table slows about 18% while held and comes back on release; the other table drifts behind.
   **Turntable layout.** Scrolling waveforms for A (top) and B (bottom) sit above the decks, like Serato or rekordbox. They show a beat grid with brighter bar lines, the playhead in the middle, ON AIR on the live table, and a readout of how many beats one copy is behind the other. Click a waveform to jump; use − / + to zoom. Each deck has a vertical pitch fader beside the platter: top is slower, like a Technics.
   **Controller.** The tables are laid out like a two-deck DJ controller: black body, gold jog wheels with an orange LED ring, backlit pads, tempo sliders on the outside edges (A left, B right) and the mixer in the middle.
   **Jog wheel (scratch).** Grab the gold top and turn it: the record follows your hand, forwards and backwards, at whatever speed you move. One full turn is 1.8 s of record (33⅓ rpm). Holding still stops the record; letting go drops it back in at speed. Drag the outer ridged ring while playing to nudge the tempo (pitch bend). Scratching runs in an AudioWorklet (`scratch-worklet.js`) with Hermite interpolation, so it sounds continuous instead of choppy.
   **Pads.** Each deck has 8 pads with two modes. **SLOW −** pads take that table's speed down by 0.01, 0.02 … 0.08 per tap; taps add up (three −0.01 taps = 0.97×). **1.00×** resets that table. **CHOP** pads are ½ beat, 1 beat, 2 beats, 1 bar, 2 bars, hook, double up and lag. Table B starts on SLOW −, table A on CHOP.
   **Slow one side.** Under each platter: **SLOW A** (hold `Q`) and **SLOW B** (hold `P`). **SLOW TIME** sets how long the slowdown takes, from 0.02 s (a snap) to 4.00 s. **DRAG** slows the table by **DRAG DEPTH** while held and brings it back over the same time on release. **STOP** turns the motor off: the record winds down to a stop over the slow time, and the table's speed is restored for the next PLAY. The other table is not touched. Settings are remembered on this device.
3. **Pitch + voice.** The screwed voice comes from slowing the record: varispeed drops the voice with the beat, the way a turntable does. **TAPE** (under each table, 0–100%) adds dubbed-cassette character: slow wow, fast flutter, soft saturation and a rolled-off top end. It is in saved versions too. Add SYRUP for the room and DOUBLE UP for the echo. *Vocal pitch* moves only the vocals (needs the split; **SPLIT VOCALS FROM BEAT** starts one and the stems attach when it finishes). *Whole song pitch* shifts everything without changing speed.
4. **Chop it live.** PLAY, then on a line you like press **↺ RUN IT BACK** (½ beat, 1 beat, 2 beats, 1 bar, 2 bars). The off-air table starts that many beats behind the live one and the fader cuts to it, so the line plays again and the song keeps going from there. Press again to bounce back. Each run-back is measured on the record at the spot you press: ScrewShop matches the hits over the last few beats against the ones N beats earlier, so it lands on the beat even if the detected BPM is slightly off or the record drifts. Short run-backs are measured over 4 beats and scaled. If the BPM reads wrong (half or double), fix it in *Record's original BPM* or use ½× / 2×. **Repeat 2×–4×** loops the run-back that many times, then drops the song back in. **HOOK IN / HOOK OUT** mark a hook; **PLAY HOOK → DROP IN** replays it on the other table, then continues. **DOUBLE UP** plays both tables one beat apart (the echo). Keys: `Z X C V B` run back ½/1/2/4/8 beats, `H` hook, `D` drag, `[` `]` cut, `\` center.
   **Back and forth (mixer pads).** **◀ A / B ▶** cut to a table. **FLICK** (hold `F`) plays the other table only while held, like flicking the crossfader. **LAG OFF-AIR** (`G`) starts the off-air copy ½ beat, 1 beat, 2 beats or 1 bar behind the live one and keeps it silent. Then flick or cut to repeat words while the song keeps moving, which is the Screw two-copy technique. With two *different* records on A and B, cut and flick go between the songs, and a run-back jumps the live table back on itself. Shortcuts also work while a slider has focus.
5. **Make a version.** Choose **DJ Screw**, **Michael Watts** or **Slowed only** → **BUILD VERSION**. ScrewShop finds the beat grid, vocal lines and a likely hook (your HOOK marks win), plans chops on the beat grid, renders a WAV offline with the tables' speed, pitch, vocal pitch, EQ and syrup, and lists every chop. **PREVIEW**, **NEW CHOPS** (re-roll), **SAVE TO SONGS**. **RECORD LIVE MIX** captures your own performance instead. Originals are never changed.

If a table is at 1.00× when you build, the style picks a speed: about 64 BPM for Screw and 70 BPM for Watts, kept between 0.60× and 0.85×.

## Vocals + Instrumental

Load a completed split, or two stem files. Both share one linked speed (BPM shown). Double-click a track for pitch, level, bass, treble, reverb, echo and trims; M/S mute and solo. **SAVE STEM MIX AS WAV** renders it. **SCREW THESE ON THE TABLES →** sends the pair to the station, keeping the vocal pitch, so you can chop with split vocals.

## How the chop works (research)

- DJ Screw played the same record on both turntables, one about a beat behind, and flicked the crossfader so words repeat without breaking tempo. He slowed records to roughly 60–70 BPM, scratched, and ran records back to repeat phrases. Sources: [Wikipedia: Chopped and screwed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopped_and_screwed), [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/features/dj-screw-tracing-genius-chopped-n-screwed-pioneer), [UH DJ Screw collection](https://findingaids.lib.uh.edu/repositories/2/resources/14).
- Michael "5000" Watts (Swishahouse) had a crisper sound and focused chops on a word or phrase he brought back. He preferred the phrase "screwed and chopped." Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopped_and_screwed), [Texas Standard](https://texasstandard.org/stories/michael-5000-watts-dead-houston-hip-hop-dj-screw-swishahouse-chopped-screwed/).
- Local analysis of *Wanna be ah Balla Freestyle* (Slim Thug, in the library): a steady ~71 BPM the whole way. Its repeats land on the beat grid: 2 beats back at 0:07, 1 bar back at 0:30, and a 2-bar phrase run three times from 3:29. That is why run-backs here are measured in beats, not seconds.

The style recipes are an approximation of these techniques, not a copy of any tape. Hook detection is a heuristic; mark the hook yourself for best results. Pitch shifting is not formant-preserving.

All land within 4 ms (typically under 1 ms). Also checks that a run-back during HOLD TO DRAG leaves both tables at full speed.
- `.venv/Scripts/python.exe tests/screw_tables_browser.py`: waveforms draw, speed pads (−0.01 steps add up, 1.00× reset), chop pads, jog-wheel scratch (a quarter turn back = 0.45 s, with sound, drops back in), slow time 0.02–4 s, DRAG on one table only, STOP at the shortest and longest time, lag is 1.00 beat, flick and cut pads, run-back and flick with two different records, TAPE live and in a rendered version, 390px width, no page errors.
- `tests/sync_browser.py`, `tests/decks_browser.py`, `tests/workspace_browser.py` cover the decks around it.

Not tested: a physical iPhone, and full-length songs with stems in the browser. A 6-minute song with stems decodes about 400 MB of audio, which is fine on a desktop but may be too much for a phone.
